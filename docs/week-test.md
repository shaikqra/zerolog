# The week test — plan

The first honest test of ZeroLog, and the only one available right now for £0
and with no Apple Watch. Written 2026-11-02.

**The question it answers:** does this app survive daily use by a real lifter
who is not being asked to admire it?

Industry benchmark: health and fitness apps keep about **3% of users at day
30**. The first person who has to fail that test is Samee.

---

## Before you start (done)

Sideloaded builds signed with a free Apple ID **stop working after 7 days**,
and re-signing usually wipes the app's data. A week of real training would have
been destroyed by the signing process rather than by anything the app did.

So Settings now has **Your data → Save a copy / Restore from a copy**.

**Do this on day 1 and after every session:** Settings → Save a copy → email it
to yourself. Restoring never deletes anything logged since the backup; it
merges, and keeps the fuller version of any session that exists twice.

---

## The rules

1. **Log every set of every session.** Not a demo, not a sample — the real
   weight and the real reps, including the sets that went badly.
2. **Do not open it to check on it.** Open it because you are training. If you
   find yourself opening it to see whether it works, that is not the test.
3. **Never correct it for me.** If it suggests a weight that is wrong, log what
   actually happened. A tidied history teaches the engine the wrong thing and
   hides the bug.
4. **Note the moment something annoys you, while it annoys you.** Afterwards you
   will have forgotten, or worse, rationalised it.

## What to write down

One line is enough. Phone notes, a message to me, anything.

- **What did you tap that did not do what you expected?**
- **What did you want that was not there?**
- **What number looked wrong?** (Especially a suggested weight.)
- **Where did you have to think?** Anywhere you paused mid-session is friction,
  and mid-set friction is exactly what this app exists to remove.
- **Did you open it on a day you were not training?** If the week view or
  "what's next" pulled you back, that is the retention signal.

## What I will be watching for in the data

- Did the suggested weights track what you actually lifted, or drift?
- Did double progression trigger when it should have?
- Did any session get abandoned halfway?
- Did the rest timer length match what you actually took?

---

## What counts as a result

**Good:** you log a full week without being reminded, and the plan on day 7
looks like something you would have written yourself.

**Bad, and useful:** you stop after two sessions. Then the question is *why* —
too slow to log, wrong weights, boring, or you simply forgot. Each of those has
a different fix, and none of them is "build more features".

**Worst case, also useful:** it was fine but you felt nothing. That means the
phone half is table stakes and everything rests on the watch, which sharpens
the decision rather than clouding it.

---

## What this does NOT test

Automatic rep counting. That is the differentiator and it still needs a watch,
a developer account, and a gym floor. **This week tests the half that competes
with Hevy; it cannot tell us whether the half that beats Hevy works.**

Both matter. This one is free and available today, which is the only reason it
goes first.
